## 1. Brand and Audience Decisions

I chose the name **Elsewhere** with the slogan **"Less ordinary. More you."** The brand focuses on showing how a simple white T-shirt can become a way for people to express their individuality.

- **Primary audience:** Young creatives who use fashion to show their personality.
- **Secondary audience:** People who value simple, versatile clothing.
- **Primary archetype:** Sage.
- **Supporting archetype:** Creator.
- **Brand voice:** Creative, playful, and rebellious.
- **Visual direction:** Postmodernism.

I wanted Elsewhere to communicate that simplicity does not have to mean ordinary. A white T-shirt can be a basic wardrobe item while still allowing someone to express their personal style.

## 2. Visual Design Decisions

I chose Postmodernism because I wanted the brand to feel creative, unconventional, and thought-provoking. The Sage archetype influenced the brand's focus on ideas, meaning, and self-expression, while the Creator archetype inspired originality and artistic freedom.

The white T-shirt provides a simple canvas that allows designs and personal styling choices to stand out without unnecessary distractions.

The color palette includes:

| Color | Hex value |
|---|---|
| Paper | `#F6F3EF` |
| Ink | `#252027` |
| Forest | `#334433` |
| Plum | `#684568` |
| Sage | `#A9B9A0` |
| Lavender | `#D8C7DB` |
| Soft | `#E5DED5` |
| Muted | `#625D63` |

I chose these colors to make the simple white shirt feel more expressive. The colors add personality to the brand's presentation without taking away from the simplicity of the product.

## 3. What I Learned About Audiences, Branding, and Design

I learned that successful branding requires understanding what appeals to a target audience and maintaining a consistent identity. Elements such as colors, typography, imagery, and brand personality influence how people perceive a brand.

I also learned that design is not only about appearance. It communicates a message and helps create a connection with an audience. The same product can be presented in different ways depending on the audience and the identity a brand wants to establish.

## 4. What I Learned from Working with AI

Working with AI helped me generate ideas, explore different design directions, and refine my concepts more efficiently. It allowed me to experiment with visual styles, slogans, layouts, and brand messaging.

However, I learned that AI suggestions still require human judgment. I had to review the suggestions, decide which ideas matched my vision, and make the final creative decisions myself.

## 5. Challenges and How I Worked Through Them

One challenge I experienced was getting AI to fully understand my ideas and provide the specific help I needed. Sometimes, AI would misunderstand my vision or generate suggestions that did not fully match what I had in mind.

I learned that communicating with AI requires clear and detailed instructions. The more thoroughly I explained my ideas, expectations, and design preferences, the more useful and accurate the responses became. This taught me the importance of being specific, reviewing AI-generated suggestions, and making my own decisions throughout the creative process.

## 6. Ethical Persuasion and Customer Experience

One approach I explored was **reciprocity** through optional styling inspiration. The idea is to give visitors useful suggestions for styling their white T-shirt with clothing they may already own.

This supports the brand by offering something helpful rather than relying only on encouraging a purchase.

A second ethical persuasion approach is still being developed. I am considering optional style preferences that could help visitors explore different ways to wear the product. This should remain a proposed idea until it is implemented and reviewed.
